# Grammar / scanner / query audit — 2026-07-25

A measured survey of where `src/parser.c` spends its bytes, which
deduplications pay, and where the scanner/grammar boundary belongs. Every
experiment below was applied to a clean tree, regenerated, validated against the
corpus and the oracle file, and measured. Results are recorded whether or not
they were kept — several were regressions, and a record of what does not work is
the point of the exercise.

The outcome is summarised in [§7](#7-outcome); the changes it describes are
landed.

### Where the parser.c bytes actually are

Counting designated initialisers in the `ts_parse_table` / `ts_small_parse_table`
region: **389 880 entries across 673 distinct symbols.**

The distribution is extremely top-heavy. The 40 highest-cost symbols each occupy
4 034–4 152 entries — roughly one entry per large state — and together account for
about **42 % of the whole table**. Every one of them is a statement-start token:

```
 4152  sym_comment            4077  anon_sym_bind        4077  sym_cmd_plot_kw
 4152  anon_sym_POUND2        4077  anon_sym_call        4077  sym_cmd_splot_kw
 4137  anon_sym_BANG          4077  anon_sym_do          4077  sym_kw_cmd_bare
 4136  anon_sym_array         4077  anon_sym_if          4077  sym_kw_cmd_optexpr
 4118  sym_identifier         4077  anon_sym_while       4077  sym_kw_cmd_exit
 ...  (40 symbols in this band)
```

This is the structural cost the grammar.js comment at line 863 already describes:
expression tails carry the statement-start follow set, and that set is replicated
across all 4 152 large states. Nonterminals (`sym_*`) contribute 106 451 entries
(27.3 %).

The practical consequence, which governs every ranking below: **the unit of parser.c
cost is one statement-start token ≈ 4 077 table entries ≈ 208 KB of parser.c.**
Anything that does not change the number of statement-start tokens, or the number of
large states, is close to free — in both directions.

## 2. Grammar dedup

`src/grammar.json` was walked with a throwaway script that hashes every normalised
sub-expression (graphify is rule-level and cannot see inside rule bodies).

### 2.1 Twenty-six rules with byte-identical bodies

Two option-rule shapes coexist in `_argument_set_show`, for no discernible reason:

```js
// Pattern A (~45 rules): head keyword inside the named rule
angles: ($) => prec.right(seq(key("angles", 2, "opt"), optional(seq($._gval_sep, $._gopts)))),

// Pattern B (26 rules): head keyword inlined at the call site, rule is the bare body
seq(key("encoding", 3, "opt"), field("arg_opts", optional($.encoding))),
encoding: ($) => seq($._gval_sep, $._gopts),
```

All 26 Pattern-B rules have one of exactly two bodies:

- `seq($._gval_sep, $._gopts)` — 21 rules: `encoding fit hidden3d history isosamples
  isosurface jitter locale mouse offsets origin output print psdir rgbmax samples size
  surface theta timefmt zero`
- `seq($._gval_sep, $._gopts_style)` — 5 rules: `errorbars format termoption timestamp
  walls`

**Experiment A (applied, measured, reverted).** The 26 rules were replaced by two
hidden rules (`_gopt_body`, `_gopt_body_style`) aliased back to the original names at
each call site.

| Metric | Baseline | After | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `STATE_COUNT` | 11 647 | 11 623 | −24 |
| `LARGE_STATE_COUNT` | 4 152 | 4 148 | −4 |
| `SYMBOL_COUNT` | 760 | 736 | −24 |
| `TOKEN_COUNT` | 505 | 505 | 0 |
| `parser.c` | 25 973 154 B | 25 922 809 B | **−50 345 B (−0.19 %)** |

`src/node-types.json` diffs empty — the public CST is unchanged. Corpus 159/159,
pruebadefuego 0 errors.

**Verdict: take it for maintainability, not for size.** Twenty-six rules collapse to
two and the two option patterns become one, but the win is 0.19 %. These rules were
already nearly free because they share the `_gopts` sub-automaton rather than cloning
it — which is the documented "alias the shared body, never clone it" rule working
as designed.

### 2.2 Repeated sub-expressions

The scan surfaced no other clone worth acting on. The highest-cost repeats are all
deliberate structure, not duplication:

| Repeat | Count | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| `seq($._gval_sep, $._gopts)` | 43 | the generic-body gate; the point of the architecture |
| `optional(seq($._gval_sep, $._gopts))` | 22 | same |
| `optional(seq($._gval_sep, $._gopts_style))` | 18 | same |
| `seq(alias(/off(s(e(t)?)?)?/, offset), $.position)` | 4 | all four are `offsetPos($)` calls already |
| `["(", sep(",", choice(assignment, _expression)), ")"]` | 3 | `cmd_if` / `using` — different precedence contexts |
| number-token internals | ~8 | one token, no sharing possible |

The `dataSeparator` / `fillStyleOpt` / `atPos` / `offsetPos` helpers exist and are
used (2, 2, 1 and 4 call sites). Their inline `(×6)` / `(×6)` / `(×9)` call-count
comments were stale and have been dropped; the counts drift with every edit and
the helpers are greppable.

## 3. Over-engineering

Little to cut. The grammar is dense but not speculative.

- **Dead constants.** `K.t` (`reg("top", 1)`) and `K.b` (`reg("bottom", 1)`) at

- **`prec.dynamic(-1)` identifier fallback** in `_argument_set_show` (line 987) and

  opportunity: inlining any of them deletes a node type downstream tooling captures.
- No unreachable rules were found (`comment` shows 0 references because it is an
  `extras` entry).

## 4. Scanner / grammar boundary

The hypothesis under test was that keywords currently living in scanner.c tables
belong in grammar.js. Both directions were measured with a real A/B on the same
keywords.

### 4.1 Promoting scanner keywords into the grammar — refuted

**Experiment B1 (applied, measured, reverted).** Ten `KW_G_FLAG` rows
(`boxed vertical invert front back noborder polar square altdiagonal bentover`) were
deleted from `GOPT_KWS` and re-expressed as `key(...)` literals in `_gopt_item`.
Same ten words, same accepted language — corpus 159/159, pruebadefuego 0 errors, so
the comparison is apples to apples.

| Metric | Baseline | After | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TOKEN_COUNT` | 505 | 515 | +10 |
| `SYMBOL_COUNT` | 760 | 770 | +10 |
| `LARGE_STATE_COUNT` | 4 152 | 4 214 | +62 |
| `parser.c` | 25 973 154 B | 26 820 280 B | **+847 126 B (+3.26 %)** |
| generate max RSS | 1.709 GB | 1.916 GB | **+12 %** |

That is **+84.7 KB of parser.c and ~+21 MB of generate RAM per keyword**, for ten
keywords out of a table of roughly 540.

Extrapolating linearly, moving all of `GOPT_KWS` into the grammar costs on the order
of **+46 MB of parser.c (a 2.8× parser) and pushes generate RAM past 10 GB.** The
extrapolation is conservative — token-set growth of that magnitude is superlinear in
practice, since generate RAM tracks the lookahead bitset width (shrink-law 2).

The tier system is not incidental complexity: it is the mechanism that keeps ~540
keywords from each buying a column in every lookahead set. **The boundary should not
move in this direction.**

### 4.2 Demoting grammar literals into the scanner — confirmed, small

The reverse direction pays, where the continuation is identical.

`cmd_opt_expr` (grammar.js:414) reads
`choice(alias($.kw_cmd_optexpr, "cmd"), alias("warn", "cmd"))` — the literal `warn`
sits beside the collapsed scanner token with exactly the same continuation, so it is a
statement-start token that buys nothing.

**Experiment B2 (applied, measured, reverted).** `{"warn", 4, KW_CMD_OPTEXPR}` added
to `CMD_KWS`; the literal dropped from grammar.js.

| Metric | Baseline | After | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TOKEN_COUNT` | 505 | 504 | −1 |
| `SYMBOL_COUNT` | 760 | 759 | −1 |
| `LARGE_STATE_COUNT` | 4 152 | 4 113 | −39 |
| `parser.c` | 25 973 154 B | 25 765 321 B | **−207 833 B (−0.80 %)** |

`node-types.json` diffs empty; corpus 159/159; `warn "boom"` and bare `warn` both
still parse as `cmd_opt_expr`. `warn` takes no abbreviation in gnuplot 6.0.4 (`war`
errors), so `min_chars` 4 is correct.

**Remaining candidate:** `printerr` (grammar.js:823) sits in the same shape beside
`cmd_print_kw`. Expected win, one more token, ≈ −208 KB.

**Non-candidates, checked and rejected:**

- `system` / `!` cannot merge into one token: `!` is also unary NOT (`PREC.UNARY`,
  line 2461) and postfix factorial (line 2468). Different continuations — shrink-law 4.
- `bind`, `call`, `import`, `array`, `do`, `if`, `while`, `else`, `test`, `stats`,
  `reset` each have a distinct continuation and would need their own scanner token.
  Same token count either way; no win, only indirection.

**Realistic total from this direction: 2 tokens, ≈ −0.4 MB (−1.6 %).**

### 4.3 Boundary verdict

Leave the boundary where it is, minus the two `warn` / `printerr` demotions. The
scanner is carrying the load it was designed to carry, and moving keywords the other
way is the single most expensive change available in this codebase.

## 5. Query audit

`tree-sitter query queries/highlights.scm` compiles clean against pruebadefuego — no
dead captures. Coverage was computed by diffing `src/node-types.json` (175 named,
193 anonymous types) against the captures in all three query files.

### 5.1 Taxonomy: highlights.scm is correct, the docs are stale

Two captures appear to contradict the tier taxonomy table in `CLAUDE.local.md`:

| Alias | `CLAUDE.local.md` | highlights.scm | Line |
|---|---|---|---|
| `flag` | `@keyword.modifier` | `@keyword.directive` | 67 |
| `plt_st` | `@function.builtin` | `@attribute` | 314 |

The authoritative table is the one in `../gnuplot-lsp/docs/backlog-context.md`
(lines 22–37), because it also defines the LSP semantic-token legend and is the input
to the highlight/semantic-token parity check. It records `flag` → `@keyword.directive`
→ `enum` and `plt_st` → `@attribute` → `function` — matching highlights.scm.

`backlog-context.md` contradicts *itself*: its prose at lines 89–91 repeats the
`@keyword.modifier` / `@function.builtin` pair. `CLAUDE.local.md` copied that prose.

**So highlights.scm needs no change here.** The stale text is `CLAUDE.local.md`'s
taxonomy table and `backlog-context.md` lines 89–91, both of which should be corrected
to match the authoritative table.

`cmd`, `opt`, `arg`, `axis`, `mod`, `coord`, `attr`, `NaN` are consistent everywhere.

### 5.2 Uncaptured anonymous node types (8)

| Node | Source | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| `b`, `t` | pm3d `at` layer spec, grammar.js:1423 `choice("b", "s", "t")` | **gap** — the sibling `"s"` *is* captured (`@variable.member`, line 224). Asymmetric by accident. |

### 5.3 Uncaptured named node types

147 named types carry no capture, but the great majority are correct: structural
containers (`source_file`, `binary_expression`, `position`, `colorspec`) and the ~85
option rules, whose head keyword is already highlighted through its `opt` alias. The
node itself should not be captured.

Six were examined against the grammar structure and gnuplot 6.0.4's own help
text. Three are correctly uncaptured; one needs a direct capture; two are
existing captures sitting in the wrong tier.

| Node | Verdict |
|---|---|
| `system` | **No capture — correct as is.** Every child is a `coord`-aliased keyword already captured by `"coord" @keyword.directive`. The rule is a pure container; the grammar comment at line 2302 says so. |
| `complex` | **No capture — correct as is.** `{Re, Im}` with `_expression` children; the braces are `@punctuation.bracket` and the operands highlight as their own literals. A node-level capture would override them. |
| `def_var` | **No highlights capture.** `seq($.identifier, "=", $._var_rhs)` spans the whole statement; capturing it would colour the RHS. The identifier child is already `@variable`. What the LSP actually wants here is scope data — `(def_var (identifier) @local.definition.var)` in a **`locals.scm`**, which this repo does not have. Separate piece of work, tracked as such. |
| `columnheader` | **`(columnheader) @function.builtin`.** gnuplot's help: `title columnheader \| title columnheader(N)` — a builtin function usable bare or called. The parenthesised form already highlights via the `#any-of?` list on `(function name: (identifier))`; the bare form is this named node and currently renders plain. Not a tier member — it is aliased to a named node, not to a tier, so it needs no `keywords.json` slot. |
| `endcondition` | **Wrong tier — should be `mod`.** `choice("keypress","button1","button2","button3","close","any")`. gnuplot: "The possible end conditions are `keypress`, `button1`, `button2`, `button3`, `close`, and `any`" — enumerated values of `pause mouse`, which the taxonomy assigns to `mod` (`@constant` / `enumMember`). All six literals are currently captured `@variable.member` in the big list at highlights.scm:175–183, i.e. tagged as option names. Fix is in grammar.js (alias the literals `mod`), then drop them from that list. |
| `fontspec` | **No node capture, but the `font` keyword is in the wrong tier.** `seq("font", field("font", $._expression))` — the value is an expression that already highlights as a string. The literal `"font"` is captured `@keyword.directive` at highlights.scm:60, which is the `coord` / toggle bucket; `font` is an option-body suboption that takes a value, so it is `arg` (`@variable.member` / `property`). Fix is in grammar.js (alias it `arg`), then drop `"font"` from the `@keyword.directive` list. |

Both tier fixes touch grammar.js, so they carry a `keywords.json` regeneration
(`npm run gen:keywords`) and change LSP semantic tokens for those words.

## 6. Findings, ranked by measured win

| # | Change | Measured win | Blast radius | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Correct the stale taxonomy text in `CLAUDE.local.md` and `backlog-context.md` lines 89–91 | correctness, 0 bytes | docs only | none — highlights.scm is already right |
| 2 | Capture `*`, `<<`, `$vgridname`, `b`/`t` | correctness, 0 bytes | highlights.scm | low |
| 3 | `(columnheader) @function.builtin` | correctness, 0 bytes | highlights.scm, 1 line | low |
| 3b | Re-tier `endcondition` literals and `font` to `mod` / `arg` | correctness, 0 bytes | grammar.js + highlights.scm + `keywords.json` | medium — changes LSP semantic tokens for those words |
| 4 | Demote `warn` + `printerr` into `CMD_KWS` | **−0.4 MB (−1.6 %)**, −2 tokens | grammar.js, scanner.c, `keywords.json` | low — CST identical, corpus green |
| 5 | Collapse the 26 clone option rules | −50 KB (−0.19 %), −24 rules, −24 symbols | grammar.js, 26 rules + 26 call sites | low — node-types byte-identical |
| 6 | Delete dead `K.t` / `K.b` | 0 bytes | grammar.js, 2 lines | none |
| 7 | Drop stale `(×6)` / `(×9)` helper comments; refresh CLAUDE.local.md metrics | 0 bytes | comments only | none |

Combined size effect of everything above: **≈ −1.8 %.**

### Left open

- **`locals.scm` does not exist in this repo.** `def_var` (§5.3) is the first
  concrete case for one: scope-aware go-to-definition wants it, highlighting does
  not. Outside the scope of this audit.
- **highlights.scm carries ~22 `TODO: decide and collapse` markers.** Each is a
  literal sitting in a flat `@variable.member` or `@attribute` bucket rather than
  in a decided tier. The two resolved here (`pause` end conditions, `font`) are
  the pattern the rest should follow.

### Resolved during the audit

**`reg("right", 2)` in `style_opts` is a deliberate guard — keep it.** Collapsing it
to `K.r` = `reg("right", 1)`, matching `label_opts`, was applied and measured:

| Metric | Baseline | After | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `STATE_COUNT` | 11 647 | 12 011 | +364 |
| `parser.c` | 25 973 154 B | 26 814 660 B | +841 506 B (+3.24 %) |
| corpus | 159 / 159 | **158 / 159** | 1 failure |
| pruebadefuego | 0 errors | **2 errors** | regression |

The failing case is `plot x with filledcurves r=10` (test/corpus/commands.txt:562).
`style_opts` is reachable inside a plot element, so a 1-char `r` collides with the
`filledcurves` radius keyword; `label_opts` is not, which is why the same minimum is
safe there. The asymmetry is correct and the two sites must not be unified.

### Not recommended

- **Moving `GOPT_KWS` into grammar.js** — measured at +3.26 % parser.c and +12 %
  generate RAM for ten keywords (§4.1). The rest of the table would be catastrophic.
- **Renaming any tier alias.** `cmd`, `opt`, `arg`, `flag`, `mod`, `coord`, `attr`,
  `axis`, `plt_st` are consumed by `../gnuplot-lsp` as semantic-token input. A rename
  is a breaking change for that repo, independent of its cost here.
- **Revisiting the `_eos` terminator redesign.** Already refuted at +44–48 % states
  across four variants. The 42 % statement-start cost measured in §1 is what that
  redesign was aimed at; it remains the largest single cost in the parser and it
  remains unaddressable by that route.

## 7. Outcome

| Metric | Before | After | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `STATE_COUNT` | 11 647 | 11 614 | −33 |
| `LARGE_STATE_COUNT` | 4 152 | 4 068 | −84 |
| `SYMBOL_COUNT` | 760 | 730 | −30 |
| `TOKEN_COUNT` | 505 | 500 | −5 |
| `parser.c` | 25 973 154 B | 25 427 416 B | **−545 738 B (−2.10 %)** |
| corpus | 159 / 159 | 162 / 162 | +3 cases |
| pruebadefuego | 0 errors | 0 errors | — |

Landed, in order: the three missing query captures; the 26 duplicate option
bodies collapsed behind two hidden rules, plus dead `K.t` / `K.b` and the
unreachable `"$vgridname"` branch; the `set pm3d at bst` fix; the `pause` end
conditions and `font` moved to their correct tiers (which also exported seven
previously invisible words to `keywords.json`); and `warn` / `printerror`
demoted into `CMD_KWS`.

Three defects surfaced that the audit was not looking for: `set pm3d at bst`
produced an ERROR node while the space-separated form gnuplot rejects parsed
cleanly; `printerror` was rejected outright; and the `cmd_stats` `"$vgridname"`
branch was unreachable. All three are fixed.

The corpus was also checked for redundancy — 162 test blocks, zero duplicate
inputs — so no consolidation was warranted.

## 8. Where the remaining size is

There is no large parser-size win available inside the current architecture. The
levers found here total roughly 1.8 %. The dominant cost — 42 % of the parse table
spent replicating the statement-start follow set across 4 152 large states — is
structural, and the one redesign that targets it is already measured as a regression.

The productive framing for this pass is therefore: **the queries have real
correctness bugs worth fixing, the grammar has a genuine dedup worth taking for
maintainability, and the parser is close to as small as this design gets.**
